Updated 10 October 2026

HTB CAP · LINUX · DOCUMENTED LAB RETROSPECTIVE

The case at a glance

Problem
A download exposes traffic from another lab session.
Decision
Compare objects, check credential reuse and inspect effective privileges.
Evidence
The guide documents distinct captures, FTP authentication, SSH access and Python CAP_SETUID.
Result
SSH access as nathan and a change to UID 0, as described in the guide.
Limit
HTB retrospective: no original PCAP or independent transcript; no verified remediation.

1. Ask whether the capture belongs to the account

The guide describes /data/1 as an empty personal capture and /data/0 as an earlier capture containing 72 packets. That specific comparison supports an object-level authorization question.

Boundary: it does not establish access to every capture or the current behavior of a live service.

Source: capture comparison (Spanish)

2. Correlate the exposed data with a service

The documented traffic contains FTP authentication information. The guide then describes access through SSH. A value observed in one protocol becomes a new hypothesis to check; the protocol alone does not prove reuse elsewhere.

Boundary: the public dossier does not include the original PCAP or an independent session transcript. Secrets are omitted.

Source: traffic analysis (Spanish)Source: SSH access (Spanish)

3. Explain escalation through an effective capability

The guide shows cap_setuid on /usr/bin/python3.8 and a final UID of 0. That permission explains the identity change more precisely than referring to a generic privilege-escalation exploit.

Proposed remediation: remove unnecessary interpreter capabilities and check the remaining service privileges. The published material does not establish that this fix was applied or retested.

Source: capability and UID check (Spanish)
